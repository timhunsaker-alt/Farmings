Four breeds: **Merino** (fine wool), **Suffolk** (meat), **Dorper** (meat, a hair sheep that sheds) and **East Friesian** (milk). They
spawn wild, follow wheat or hay, and are tamed in a [corral](corral). House them in a [sheep shed](sheep-shed).

## Breeding
- A ewe lamb is first bred at **7** months and **65%** of mature weight (**70%** for Merino).
- Gestation is **147** days. Lambs per lambing: Merino **1.25**, Dorper **1.5**, Suffolk **1.8**, East Friesian **2.2**.
- Ewes cycle only in their breeding season. Day 0 of a world is **1** October, the middle of the season, so a new flock can breed at
  once. Out of season a ewe reads ANOESTROUS.
- A ram serves ewes in heat in the same pen or pasture, or use a ram's [straw](breeding).

## Wool
- A full fleece grows in **365** days and is ready to shear at **300** days.
- Shear at the [shearing shed](shearing-shed), or with shears (one grade lower).
- Fineness sets the grade: a Merino fleece is fine, a Suffolk quarter blood. Spin it at the [wool mill](wool-mill).

## Milk and meat
- East Friesian ewes give **365** kg of milk over **221** days, milked in the [parlour's](parlour) sheep stalls.
- Sheep under a year give **lamb chops**, older ones **mutton**.
